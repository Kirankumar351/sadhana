AI LAYER

Eleven AI features, one rule

AI drafts. A person decides. Nothing an AI produced about a date, a fee or an eligibility criterion ever reaches a student without a human reading it first — because the cost of being wrong here is somebody's year.

The one rule, stated plainly. Everywhere else in edtech, AI output goes straight to the user. Here it cannot, for one specific class of content: eligibility, dates, fees and anything a person will act on to apply for a job. For explanation, practice and study help, AI answers directly — but always grounded in our own indexed corpus, always labelled, and always with a route back to the official source.

What AI is never allowed to do

State an eligibility outcomeThe deterministic engine decides that, not a model
Publish a date or a feeHuman verification against the official PDF, every time
Answer without sourcesNo retrieved passages means no answer
Predict a cutoffWe show real historical data instead
Promise selectionBlocked at the prompt and in output filtering
Reproduce copyrighted booksGeneration is grounded only in our own legal corpus

How grounding works

Student question (Telugu or English) │ ▼ Intent router ── eligibility / dates? ──► deterministic engine, never the model │ │ study, concept, revision ▼ Retrieval over our own corpus only: · exam syllabi and patterns · notification facts · previous papers and answer keys · approved material · answered community doubts · current affairs digests │ ▼ found nothing relevant? ──► "I don't have this. Ask the community." (no free generation) │ ▼ Generation with the retrieved passages + glossary + Telugu style guide │ ▼ Output checks · no eligibility claims · no invented dates · no guarantee language · sources present │ ▼ Answer, labelled AI, with the source cards it used │ └─► every answer carries "Report a wrong answer"
Retrieval-grounded, not free-form. The model may only answer from what we have indexed. This is what keeps a study assistant from confidently inventing an exam pattern that does not exist — the single most damaging thing a product like this can do.

Labelling

Every AI output carries the violet AI mark. Users always know what a machine wrote. We never blur that line to make the product look smarter.

Confidence floor

Below the threshold the assistant says so and routes to the community rather than guessing. A visible "I don't know" builds more trust than a fluent wrong answer.

Cost discipline

Aggressive caching, small models for routing, per-user daily caps. AI cost per active user stays under ₹0.40 a month, or the free product does not work.

1. The idea of Telangana, 1948–1970

The demand for a separate Telangana state has roots in the merger of the Hyderabad State with the Indian Union in 1948 and the subsequent formation of Andhra Pradesh in 1956.

Signed on 20 February 1956, the Gentlemen's Agreement provided for a Regional Council, reservation of posts for Telangana candidates, and the retention of surplus Telangana revenues within the region. Its incomplete implementation became the central grievance of the movement.

The four things that keep this affordable

1Cache aggressively by content, not by user

2Small model for routing and filtering
The exam-relevance classifier reads 412 articles a day. Using a large model there would cost 20× for no gain.
3Hard per-user caps
30 questions a day covers real study. It also stops one user costing what a thousand do.
4Retrieval keeps prompts short
Two retrieved passages beat stuffing a whole syllabus into context — cheaper and more accurate at the same time.

Why a single gateway. Cost caps, refusal logging, prompt versioning and output filtering all live in one place. If any feature could call the API directly, every one of those guarantees becomes optional — and the one that gets skipped will be the one that mattered.

The grounded-answer prompt, in shape

SYSTEM You answer questions for Telugu-speaking Indian government exam aspirants. Answer ONLY from the passages provided below. If they do not contain the answer, say so plainly and suggest asking the community. Never fill a gap from general knowledge. NEVER: - state whether a person is eligible for anything - give a date, fee or deadline that is not in the passages - predict a cutoff, a rank or a selection chance - promise or imply that following advice leads to selection Answer in {locale}. Apply this glossary exactly — these are the words people search for: {glossary}. Keep numerals in Latin script. Be direct. An aspirant is short of time and under pressure. Cite which passage each fact came from. USER Passages: {retrieved_passages} Question: {question}
The prompt is the weakest of the three layers. A user can talk around instructions. That is why the eligibility route, the retrieval gate and the output filters are enforced in application code — the prompt only has to handle the honest majority.

Build order

PhaseShipWhy this order
NowNotification extraction · TranslationAlready in the plan. They pay for themselves in content velocity from week one.
M4+Question generator · Current affairs pipelineCurrent affairs is the binding constraint on the daily quiz, and the daily quiz is the retention engine. This unblocks the thing the whole business rests on.
M5Corpus embedding · Ask Sadhana · Explain simplerNeeds a corpus worth retrieving from, so it comes after exam pages and material exist. Explain is cheap and cached — ship it alongside.
Phase 2Doubt solver · Flashcards · Notes generatorDoubt solver only makes sense once the community exists. Flashcards and notes deepen the material library.
Phase 3Study plan · Answer evaluation · Mock interviewEvaluation and interview are Group 1 features and expensive per use. They belong behind premium, where the users who need them will pay.
Do not build AI before the corpus exists. A retrieval assistant with nothing to retrieve gives confident empty answers, and that first impression is very hard to undo. Exam pages, syllabi and past papers come first — the assistant is only as good as what it can read.
