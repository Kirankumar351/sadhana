The data spine

Before wiring AI into anything, one question has to be answered for every fact in the product: where does it live, who is allowed to write it, and who reads it. Get this wrong and the same number ends up in three places, drifting apart.

The rule that makes this a commercial product rather than a demo. Every fact has exactly one owner table. AI never becomes an owner — it only proposes into a draft table, and a human write promotes it. If an AI feature needs a fact nobody owns, that is a schema gap, not a prompt problem.

How the corpus stays honest

OWNER TABLES DERIVED SERVED exams.syllabus ─┐ exam_cutoffs ─┤ notifications ─┼──► ai_chunks ──────► retrieval ──► AI answer materials ─┤ (embedded) │ questions ─┤ │ posts + answers ─┤ ▼ news_items ─┘ no chunk = no answer Any write to an owner table fires ReindexChunks for that record. Delete an owner row and its chunks go with it — no orphans, so the assistant can never quote a notification that was cancelled.
This is the single most important wiring decision in the whole AI layer. The corpus is derived, never authored. It means a correction made once in the admin panel propagates to every AI answer within a minute — and it means nobody can ever "fix" an AI answer by editing something the rest of the product does not see.

Where AI appears in the user portal

Not a separate "AI" tab. Each feature sits inside the screen where the need already exists, so a person meets it at the moment it helps rather than having to go looking for it.

The mistake to avoid. Putting everything behind an "AI" menu item makes AI the product. It is not — the product is finding a job and preparing for it. AI is how some of those screens get better. Only one feature earns its own destination, and that is the assistant.

Why AI is its own group, not scattered

Because the permission boundary is different. A content editor can draft a notification but must not be able to change a prompt or read the cost dashboard. Grouping the pipelines lets the whole group be gated behind Content lead and Owner, which is one rule instead of five.

What this reveals — 1

Nine of twelve features read ai_chunks. It is the single hardest dependency in the AI layer, and nothing works before it exists and is populated.

What this reveals — 2

glossary is read by ten features but was only ever designed as an admin screen with no table behind it. It needs to become real, first.

What this reveals — 3

Only one feature writes directly to a user-facing table without review: the doubt solver, into answers. That is why it needs an is_ai flag and a rank that keeps it below every human answer.

Four flows, traced end to end

Each one crosses the admin portal, the database and the user portal. If a step has no owner, no screen or no table, it shows up here.

Audit — twelve gaps

Found by tracing every AI feature against the schema in Volume 2 and the screens in both portals. Five are blocking, four need a screen, three are policy holes.

Blocking

1
The retrieval corpus does not exist

Nine of the twelve AI features read ai_chunks, and Volume 2's schema has no such table, no embedding column and no reindex job. Every retrieval feature is blocked on this one object.

Fix Add ai_chunks with a vector column. Add a ReindexChunks job fired by observers on exams, notifications, materials, questions, answers and news_items. Backfill once, then keep it incremental.
2
The glossary is a screen with no table

Ten features inject the glossary into their prompt, and the translation reviewer checks compliance against it. But it was only ever designed as an admin screen — there is no glossary table, so there is nothing to inject.

Fix Create glossary(term_en, term_te, rule, category, note) and load it in PromptRegistry. This is small and it is a hard dependency for translation quality, which is the entire product thesis.
3
Nothing stores a target exam date

The study planner needs "28 days to Group 2" to allocate time. user_exam_preferences stores which exams a person follows but not which one they are actually writing next, or when.

Fix Add user_exam_preferences.target_date and is_primary. Ask for it in profile step 3 — "which one are you writing next?" — which also improves feed ranking and push relevance, not just the study plan.
4
Per-user AI caps have nowhere to count

The guardrails screen sets 30 questions a day and 30 notes a month. Nothing in the schema counts them, so the cap cannot be enforced and one user could cost what a thousand do.

Fix Count from ai_requests with an index on (user_id, feature, created_at), cached in Redis per user per day. No new table needed — but the index is not optional at this volume.
5
AI answers cannot be distinguished from human ones

The doubt solver writes into answers, which has no is_ai column. Without it the UI cannot label the answer, cannot rank it below humans, and reputation points would be awarded to a machine.

Fix Add answers.is_ai and answers.ai_request_id. Sort AI answers last regardless of votes. Exclude them from reputation and from "best answer" eligibility.

Missing screens and routes

6
Current affairs has no home

The pipeline produces a daily digest and there is no screen, no route and no nav item to publish it to. It is also the most SEO-valuable page type the product could have — daily fresh Telugu content on high-volume queries.

Fix Add /{locale}/current-affairs and /{locale}/current-affairs/{date}, a nav item, a dashboard card, and a monthly compilation PDF. Treat it as a first-class content type with its own sitemap.
7
Ask Sadhana has no entry point

It is designed as a full screen but appears in neither portal's navigation. A feature nobody can find is a feature nobody uses.

Fix Persistent icon beside search on every page, its own route, and a context-scoped "Ask about this job" button on notification detail.
8
Flashcards have no screen and no scheduler

The deck exists in the design but there is no route, no flashcards table, and no job to move cards through their intervals. Spaced repetition without a scheduler is just a list.

Fix Add the table with ease, interval_days and due_at, a nightly job to surface due cards, and a screen under the quiz tab.
9
The five AI admin screens are not in the sidebar

Newsroom, question generator, material generator, guardrails and cost have no route, no nav entry and no permission gate. They exist as designs with nowhere to live.

Fix Add an AI group to the admin sidebar, gated to Content lead and Owner, with prompt editing and guardrails restricted to Owner alone.

Policy holes

10
The push budget does not know about AI content

The cap is five pushes a day. Current affairs is a new daily push that was never counted in that five, so the real ceiling would quietly become six.

Fix Register current affairs as a push type inside PushBudget with its own user preference toggle, and decide its priority against a deadline reminder rather than adding it on top.
11
Generated material is not labelled on the public page

The material generator produces library content and the library shows "Official" or "Notes" badges only. A reader cannot tell that a page was AI-drafted and human-edited.

Fix Add materials.source_type = 'ai_assisted' and a third badge with the editor's name. Readers deserve to know, and the named editor creates accountability.
12
The DPDP export does not include AI data

DataExportService returns profile, quiz attempts, posts and saved jobs. It does not include AI conversations, generated study plans or flashcard decks — all of which are personal data the user is entitled to.

Fix Extend the export to cover ai_requests, study_plans, flashcards and answer_evaluations, and include all four in the deletion routine.
Two flows came through clean. Notification extraction to personalised alert, and translation drafting to human approval, are both fully wired — every step has an owner table, an admin screen and a user destination. They were designed with review built in from the start, which is exactly why they hold up.

ExamNotificationpublished, updated, deletedReindexChunks + purge cacheWithout it the assistant quotes a cancelled notification, or misses one published an hour ago

Materialapproved, unpublishedReindexChunksRejected material must leave the corpus, or a copyright removal is only half done

The deletion observer is the one people forget. If a material file is removed after a copyright complaint but its chunks stay in the corpus, the assistant keeps quoting it. The takedown is then incomplete in exactly the way that matters legally.

Corrected build order

Dependencies rearranged after the audit. Two AI features move earlier because they pay for themselves in content velocity; four move later because they need a corpus that does not exist yet.

The sequencing error worth naming. The original AI plan put Ask Sadhana in month 5. But Ask Sadhana reads ai_chunks, and ai_chunks is only worth building once exam pages, syllabi and past papers exist — which is the end of Sprint 1. Shipping the assistant against a thin corpus produces confident empty answers, and that first impression is very hard to undo.

The one thing to build first

The glossary table. It is half a day of work, it has no dependencies, and ten downstream features silently degrade without it. Every AI feature shipped before it exists will need reworking afterwards — and translation quality is not a detail here, it is the reason the product beats Adda247 in Telugu at all.

The one thing not to rush

The corpus. Building ai_chunks before exam pages, syllabi and past papers are populated gives you an assistant with nothing to read. It will answer anyway — fluently and emptily — and the people who try it in that state will not come back to try it again.

What this map is for. Before any AI migration, check it against the read/write matrix. Before any AI screen, check it against the placement table. The twelve gaps here were all found by tracing paper against paper — which is much cheaper than finding them in production, on a notification day, with 18,000 people on the site.
